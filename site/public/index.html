TC Jam: one phone keeps every camera in time

The first part of the app that works on real cameras (an α7 V and an α6600, 29.10.2026). Since 31.10.2026 it also lives inside Manual Camera (Mantra) (Settings → SONY): its top line shows whether the Sonys record, holding REC offers START SONY for all of them and the phone together, and every phone take carries the same free-run time code. The Sony Alpha bodies, new and used, are listed below. The phone becomes a time code generator, starts every camera together, and claps for the edit.

01

Read a camera's screen

Point the phone at any camera showing its time code, drag the box onto the numbers, pinch to zoom. The phone reads them on the phone itself (no internet) and locks its own clock to that camera: the numbers turn green when they run together.

02

Send the phone's time code over USB

Plug a camera into the phone and press PHONE TC GENERATOR > CAMERA (USB): it gets the same time code, Free Run, at the turn of a second. Works on the newer Sony bodies (α7 V, α7 IV, α1, FX3, FX30, ZV-E1 and others; the full list is under HELP in the app). The α6600 and older bodies are read from their screen instead.

03

Record together, clap together

Over Bluetooth one key starts and stops every camera, and each camera confirms it is recording. SYNC CAMERA REC BUTTONS: press REC on one camera and the others follow. CLAP (after REC, or CLAP NOW by hand): 1, 2, 3 kHz and then all three, the phone's LED flashing one frame with each.

Installing: open the download on an Android phone (64-bit, Android 10 or newer) and allow your browser to install apps. On each camera: MENU → Network → Bluetooth → Bluetooth Rmt Ctrl: On, then pair; for USB, the camera's USB connection on PC Remote (α7 V: USB PORT 1, Remote Shoot/Trn.). The version numbers only grow, in whole numbers, and this button always gives the newest. Tell Marko what worked and what did not.

The idea

One person, several cameras,
like jazz musicians

A gong bath, a concert, a talk on a boat: Marko often films alone or with a small crew, with three or more Sony cameras running and an Accsoon transmitter on each. The phone becomes the monitor and the remote for all of them.

01

The picture is sacred

The keys live in the black bands beside the 16:9 picture, never on it. A key is a word; its colour is its state.

02

One key, one camera

The lens keys of Manual Camera (Mantra) become camera keys: CAM 1, CAM 2, CAM 3. One tap and you see and steer that camera.

03

One experiment at a time

Each piece is tried on the real cameras, then kept, changed or dropped, and written in the experiment log. Nothing is built ahead.

The camera rail

The lens switcher, now a camera switcher

Same place, same logic as the L1 to L5 keys in Manual Camera (Mantra). Under each key, in a whisper, the body it is. Green is the camera you are on. Grey is connected and waiting. Dark is a key with no camera behind it. A small red dot is the camera on air.

the camera you are on connected, waiting no camera on this key on air (program)

Try it: tap CAM1, CAM2, CAM3. Tap SONY to swap the picture source, REC to roll.

Two sources per camera

Sony direct: the camera's own Wi-Fi picture and every setting (ISO, shutter, iris, white balance, tap to focus, photo, record). Accsoon: the clean HDMI picture from the CineView on the camera, with the everyday keys (record, shutter, focus) over Bluetooth at the same time. A double tap in the middle of the picture opens SOURCES, as in Mantra Monitor.

What a key does in each source

A key the current source cannot honour stays where it is and turns dark. ISO is dark in Accsoon mode, and REC still works there over Bluetooth. Your thumb always finds each key in the same place.

Multiview

Three cameras and the program, at one glance

Borrowed from OBS Studio, vMix and the ATEM switchers: the screen splits into four. Three quarters are the cameras, one quarter is what goes out. A red border and a red label mean live, on program. A green border means preview, the next shot waiting.

Try it: tap a camera to put it on preview, then CUT. In the two-camera switcher, CUT swaps preview and program.

Where program sits

Top right, as in OBS and ATEM, where the program is always on the right. The cameras read in order: CAM1 top left, then CAM2 and CAM3 along the bottom.

Tally on every tile

The camera on air carries a red outline, and the camera on preview a green one. The same red dot sits on its key in the rail, so the state is the same everywhere.

Solo or with a director

Two ways to try: preview, then CUT (a director's habit), or one tap straight to program (a solo shooter's). We keep the one that feels right in the hand.

Connections

One phone has one Wi-Fi radio

Each camera's Direct Wi-Fi and each CineView Nano is its own little network, and a phone is on one network at a time. So the newer cameras join one shared network, the phone's hotspot or a small travel router, and the phone talks to all of them there. Bluetooth is a separate radio: every camera also gets its keys over it.

Wi-Fi: picture and every setting Bluetooth: record, shutter, focus, always HDMI: the clean picture into the Accsoon

The pale Accsoons are not reached by this phone: a phone holds one Nano at a time. A Nano feeds up to four phones, so a second operator can watch any camera on their own phone.

ONE CAMERA

Straight to the camera

The phone joins the camera's Direct Wi-Fi, or the Accsoon's. The simplest start, and the first experiment.

SEVERAL CAMERAS

One shared network

The phone's hotspot or a small travel router. The A7 IV and A7 V join it as for a computer's Remote Shoot; each gets its own address and sends its picture, so the multiview fills from the network. The a6600 cannot join: it comes in through its Accsoon and Bluetooth.

ALWAYS

Bluetooth beside it

A Bluetooth link to every camera for the everyday keys. It keeps working when Wi-Fi is busy with pictures.

Which cameras

From the a6600 to the A7 V

Sony's remote protocol, Camera Remote Command, covers both. The A7 V speaks its newer PTP 3 over a cable or the network. The a6600 speaks the older PTP 2, and only over a USB cable, so on its own it is steered by cable or by Bluetooth, and its picture comes through its Accsoon.
